About QRnegie

QRnegie puts a little Carnegie Mellon in your QR codes. Add one to a course page, a campus event, a club poster or anywhere a printed image should take someone to a link.

How It Works

QRnegie draws the QR code's dots in the style you pick and leaves the patterns a scanner looks for, like the three corner squares, plain and intact.

  • Your content goes directly in the QR code. QRnegie does not add a redirect or tracking link. To change where a code goes, make a new one.
  • An exported image works on its own. Save the SVG or PNG to keep it with your project. QRnegie doesn't store your QR codes. Once you've saved or printed a code, it works without QRnegie.
  • An image address always encodes the same text. Its look can change when a design improves.

The Stories Behind the Designs

Each design has a story, and most of them start on campus. They come from the university's own Scotty artwork, from places you can still visit and from CMU's marks and its past. There are many more stories here, and room for more designs.

Scotty Portraits

Mosaic Scotty and Basic Scotty use the red-scarf portrait from University Communications & Marketing's Scotty artwork. Mosaic Scotty combines the portrait with the code's dots; Basic Scotty places it in the center.

Kraus Campo

Part of CMU's public art collection, the rooftop garden by artist Mel Bochner and landscape architect Michael Van Valkenburgh inspired the numbered tiles and orange frame.

Wean Hall

Wean Hall is said to look like a turtle eating a Cheeto. This design pairs its concrete facade with Clark Winter's orange sculpture, For the Love of Two Oranges, which stood outside the entrance for 40 years.

Binary Heart

Binary Heart puts the CMU heart among ones and zeros, giving a computing twist to “My heart is in the work.” Andrew Carnegie used those words in 1900 when he offered to establish a technical school in Pittsburgh; they became the university's motto.

Tilted Square

Tilted Square recalls the university's tilted-square logo of the 1980s and 1990s. The design celebrates The University Store's partnership with University Archives to bring vintage CMU designs back to campus.

Block Monogram

Block Monogram uses the Carnegie-Mellon block monogram as a frame, with the code taking the place of the M. The design celebrates The University Store's partnership with University Archives to bring vintage CMU designs back to campus.

Contributing to QRnegie

QRnegie is a TypeScript app built with Express. It encodes text with node-qrcode and draws each style as SVG; sharp turns it into a PNG. Tests decode the finished image of every style with jsQR, a separate reader.

The GitHub repository has the code, its tests and documentation. To report a problem or suggest a change, open an issue or send a pull request.
